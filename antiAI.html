Book Design — AI Archive

anti-ai manifesto

A printed archive of dissent — collecting, organizing, and designing the growing body of human writing that pushes back against the uncritical acceleration of artificial intelligence.

Year 2025
Role Book Design, Editorial Design, Curation
Tools InDesign, Illustrator, Research
Type Book Design — Polemical Archive

The discourse around AI moves fast and loud. The criticism of it tends to get buried — scattered across op-eds, academic papers, open letters, and essays that rarely share a page. anti-ai manifesto gathers that criticism and gives it physical form: a book that is itself an act of preservation and argument.

The archive is organized not chronologically but thematically — labor, authorship, power, aesthetics, ecology, consent. Each section opens with a short framing essay and unfolds into curated primary sources: direct quotes, excerpted arguments, and documented moments of pushback from artists, researchers, writers, and workers who have said: not like this.

"Making a book about AI felt like the right move — it's the most stubbornly human format. It doesn't update, it doesn't learn, it doesn't optimize. It just says what it says."

Design Approach

The design system is intentionally antagonistic to the visual language of tech. No gradients. No rounded corners. No friendly UI chrome. The book uses a stark two-color palette, heavy rule lines, and tight, dense typesetting that prioritizes density of argument over ease of browsing. It's meant to be read, not scanned.

Typography leans on a strong, upright serif for body text — authoritative, un-algorithmic — while section labels and marginalia use a compressed monospace that reads as both archival and slightly oppositional. The overall effect is closer to a legal brief or a broadsheet than a design portfolio piece. That's the point.

Curation & Research

Assembling the archive meant reading widely and choosing carefully — prioritizing voices that are often sidelined in the mainstream AI conversation: artists whose work has been scraped without consent, workers displaced by automation, researchers raising ethical flags that went unheeded. The book doesn't offer a single unified position. It offers the evidence, and lets the weight of it do the arguing.

There's something deliberately contradictory about a designer who works at the intersection of AI and HCI making this project. That contradiction is part of the work — a refusal to pick a clean side in a debate that doesn't have one.
